# D4 — Speakers: decoding the OEM amp driver out of the vendor kernel

**Status: ✅ working.** Clean stereo, correct left/right. This is the clearest example of the
[reverse-engineering method](../00-reverse-engineering.md): the amps' entire configuration was pulled
out of the **vendor Android kernel binary** with Ghidra/`objdump` and shipped as firmware for a
mainline driver. Patches `0003` and `0004` in [`kernel/patches/`](../../kernel/patches/).

## Hardware

Two **TI TAS5806MD** class-D amplifiers on **I²C4**: `@0x2c` (left, "tweeters") and `@0x2f` (right,
"woofers"), fed by **SAI1** as I²S (2 slots × 16-bit, MCLK 49.152 MHz). Each amp has a power-down
(PDN) GPIO and a fault GPIO on GPIO4.

**Mainline driver used:** `sound/soc/codecs/tas5805m.c` (the TAS5805M is protocol-compatible with the
TAS5806), plus the i.MX machine driver `imx-card` and `fsl_sai`. The vendor kernel had its own
built-in `snd_soc_tas5806` driver and `snd_soc_imx_tas5806` machine driver — **not** in mainline.

## The RE: pulling the amp "DSP program" out of the ELF

TAS5805/06 amps need a long "DSP program" — a sequence of register writes (biquads, mixers, gains)
generated by TI's PPC3 tool. The OEM didn't ship that as a file. Disassembling the vendor kernel
(`objdump -d` + kallsyms, cross-referenced in Ghidra/REA) showed the whole thing is **two constant
tables compiled into the kernel**:

```
registers_tweeters  @ 0xffff800011e5b9d8   (3264 bytes)  → amp 0x2c
registers_woofers   @ 0xffff800011e5c698   (3264 bytes)  → amp 0x2f
```

The driver's `tas5806_init_dsp_work()` just replays the table for the amp's I²C address. Reading its
disassembly gave the exact opcode format:

- plain `{reg, val}` byte pairs → `i2c_write(reg, val)`;
- `0xFE, N` → delay N ms;
- `0xFD` / `0xFF` pairs → skip (no I²C);
- page/book switching is just ordinary `write(0x00, page)` / `write(0x7f, book)` entries in the table.

The bytes were pulled straight from the ELF's `PT_LOAD` segment at each symbol address and
**SHA-256-verified against the binary**, then shipped as
`/lib/firmware/tas5805m_dsp_{tweeters,woofers}.bin` for the mainline driver's `ti,dsp-config-name`
mechanism. (These blobs are vendor-derived, so they're **not** in this repo — the extraction script
pulls them from *your* backup: [05-extract-vendor-blobs.md](../05-extract-vendor-blobs.md).)

The probe/hw_params disassembly also gave the rest: GPIO polarities (PDN is **active-high**: low →
pulse → high = on), the SAI format (I²S, 2 slots × 16-bit, codec is clock consumer), supported rates,
and the exact pre-stream register writes.

## The fixes (driver + device tree)

Mapping all that onto mainline needed:

1. **Ship the vendor tables as per-amp firmware** (above). Left amp ≠ right amp — different DSP
   coefficients for a 2-way system.
2. **SAI clock tree** (patch `0003`): mainline `fsl_sai` needs the clock-names
   `bus/mclk0..3/pll8k/pll11k` and the SAI1 root assigned to the audio PLL at 49.152 MHz, or every
   `hw_params` fails to derive the bit clock. This was the big "no sound at all" blocker.
3. **PDN polarity** (patch `0004`): the vendor enable GPIO is **active-high**; a mismatched polarity
   held the amps off. Fixing this is what first produced sound.
4. **S16 + TDM** (patch `0004`): the vendor streamed 16-bit into 2×16-bit TDM slots; mainline
   defaulted to S32 and mangled it. Add `SNDRV_PCM_FMTBIT_S16_LE` and set the 2-slot/16-bit TDM.
5. **Driver behaviour** (patch `0004`): load the DSP table **once** (like the vendor), clear a latched
   fault after a warm start, and **cap the volume**.

### The "distortion" was just gain

The single most time-wasting bug: after all the above, audio played but sounded distorted. The cause
wasn't the format or the DSP program — it was that a previous "turn it up to max" set the mixer to
**+48 dB** on top of a unity-gain DSP program, hard-clipping everything. Mainline's `tas5805m`
volume control goes up to +48 dB; the amps also over-current-trip above about −9 dB with these DSP
tables in a quiet room.

**Fix:** cap `TAS5805M_VOLUME_MAX` at **98 = −12 dB** (dB = index − 110), default **92 = −18 dB**.
Lesson: before concluding anything about audio quality, know exactly what your mixer index means in
dB, and test with a −12 to −20 dBFS tone, not a full-scale one.

## Using it

The speakers are exposed through ALSA as the normal `default` device (set up by the
[overlay](../../rootfs/overlay/etc/asound.conf) with `dmix` so multiple apps can mix). See the
[developer guide](../developer-guide.md) for how apps play audio, the volume table, and the
first-sound startup delay.
